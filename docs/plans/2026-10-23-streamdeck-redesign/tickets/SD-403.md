# SD-403 — Provider meters from Build.Usage, closest-to-limit account (fixes Claude "Awaiting data")

**Complexity:** 3
**Phase hint:** 1
**Area:** area:ui
**Depends on:** #5589 — PR #5618 edits `build/usage.ex`, `streamdeck_projection/meters.ex` and `art/segments.ts`, and it changes `acc` to mean remaining. This ticket must build on that (native `blocked_by`). SD-101 — the `AiurWeb.Streamdeck.Deck` shell and its channel subscription wiring.
**Serializes with:** SD-101 (`streamdeck_channel.ex` join/handle_info, `streamdeck_projection.ex`), SD-401 (`surface.ts` strip block), SD-404 and SD-405 (same `Deck.Meters` section), SD-105 (deletes `StreamdeckProjection.Meters`, `StreamdeckMeterRetention`, and the TS window classification)

## Outcome
The physical deck shows a real reading for every provider. For Claude, it shows the account
that is closest to its limit, with that account's name. The deck shows the same numbers as the
Build page Models card, and the same "left" wording after #5589. "Awaiting data" appears only
when the Build page also has no reading.

## Context and evidence
`evidence/D-findings.md` §3, §4 and §5. The daemon re-keys windows to `session`/`weekly` and drops
`duration_minutes` (`streamdeck_projection/meters.ex:134-156`). The sidecar classifies by
`duration_minutes` only (`touchStrip/providerSegment.ts:110-159`). The result is that two windows
with no duration give `hasData: false`. The emulator reads by key and shows data, so the two surfaces disagree.

## Scope

  - `Build.Usage.load(:streamdeck, meter_source: AiurWeb.Streamdeck.Deck.MeterSource)` and then

  Coalesce through the existing 250 ms flush.

- The emulator reads the same `usage` map (no change beyond the source).

## Contract and invariants
- Per window: `remaining_percent` comes from `acc` (remaining after #5589). `used_percent = 100 - remaining_percent`
  is kept so the TS reader is unchanged. `nil` stays `nil` and is never `0` or `100`.
- Closest-to-limit account: the index `i` with the lowest `min(session.acc[i], weekly.acc[i])`
  among known values. Ties go to the account name sort order. `summary_label` = `"<name> · closest of N"`. When some accounts
  are unknown, use `"<name> · k/N observed"`.
- The `reset_at` of the chosen account is the window's earliest reset (Build.Usage keeps no per-account reset; see SD-404).
- A row with `none: true` gives `state: "unknown"`, `windows: {}`, and carries the row `note` as `reason`.
- `stale: true` gives `freshness: "stale"`.

```json
"usage": {"claude": {"provider": "claude", "state": "observed", "freshness": "fresh",
   "summary_label": "work · closest of 2",
   "windows": {"session": {"remaining_percent": 12, "used_percent": 88, "resets_at": "2026-10-23T22:00:00Z"},
               "weekly":  {"remaining_percent": 40, "used_percent": 60, "resets_at": "2026-10-27T09:00:00Z"}}},
 "codex": {"provider": "codex", "state": "unknown", "windows": {}, "reason": "Awaiting first observation"}}
```

## Acceptance criteria
1. A Claude fixture with two accounts (session remaining 50 and 12, weekly remaining 70 and 40) gives
   `usage.claude.summary_label == "<second> · closest of 2"` and `windows.session.remaining_percent == 12`.
2. A Claude reading with `five_hour` and `seven_day` windows and no `duration_minutes` gives
   `providerSegmentModel(...).hasData === true` in TS, with session `usedPercent` 88.
3. A provider row with `none: true` gives `state: "unknown"` and no windows. The TS strip prints
   "Awaiting data" for it, and never "0%".
4. The physical strip and the emulator show the same percent for each provider for one fixture.
5. A `{:provider_meter_changed, snapshot}` message gives one `usage` push within about 300 ms.
