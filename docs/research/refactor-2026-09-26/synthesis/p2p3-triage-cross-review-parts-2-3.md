## Shared decision rule needed

Part 2 records **139/297 source-supported** and **13/297 fix**; part 3 records **279/295 source-supported** and **135/295 fix**. The populations differ, so these rates alone do not prove an error. The examples below do show a substantive threshold mismatch: part 2 often leaves a directly visible static mechanism `unknown/defer`, while part 3 calls an analogous static mechanism `source-supported/fix` with an explicit caveat that runtime incidence is unproved. A consistent rule should separate (a) source support for the mechanism, (b) observed incidence or impact, and (c) whether the proposed fix is ready. Missing runtime evidence should not automatically turn a source-supported mechanism into `unknown`; conversely, `fix` should identify the behavioral test and owner needed before implementation.
